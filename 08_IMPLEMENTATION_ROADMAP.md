# TKO Consulting Business Roadmap

**Objective:** Close the first $100,000 consulting engagement in 30 days using existing repository assets
**Engineering posture:** No production engineering required to sell or start the engagement
**Sequence:** Commercial risk clearance → packaged proof → qualified conversations → phase-gated proposal → delivery

## Executive recommendation

Do not build a new assessment engine, dashboard, scoring system, generalized platform, BoundOS product, or TIF feature before the first sale.

Package the existing:

- $25,000 Prior Authorization Performance Diagnostic;
- $65,000–$95,000 90-Day Improvement Sprint;
- assessment and interview libraries;
- sample deliverables;
- RachelOS proof;
- current site and qualified intake;
- TIF evidence discipline.

The most direct $100,000 offer is a two-phase **Prior Authorization Performance Recovery Program**:

```text
Phase 1: 15-day Diagnostic              $25,000
Decision gate: continue / internal / partner / defer / stop
Phase 2: 90-day Improvement Sprint      $75,000
Total authorized program              $100,000
```

One master SOW may simplify procurement, but Phase 2 must remain evidence-gated and separately authorized.

RachelDelray provides a direct warning for this roadmap: capability production can outpace deployment, operator use, and outcome evidence. Its current top priorities are clearing an existing message backlog, serving an already-verified deployment, and recording outcomes—not engineering. TKO should impose the same discipline on itself: no new consulting platform or assessment automation while the existing offer, proof, and buyer conversations remain underused.

## 30-day commercial plan

### Days 0–3 — Clear authority and risk

#### Actions

- Confirm outside-business, employment, current-client, IP, confidentiality, non-solicitation, and conflict-of-interest boundaries.
- Record which healthcare experience may be described and which current employer / client facts may never enter TKO evidence.
- Define the standard data posture: no PHI through the website, de-identified evidence by default, no production access in standard scope, retention / deletion terms, and whether a BAA can be considered.
- Confirm the current offer name, price, scope, and two-phase $100,000 packaging.
- Establish a target account / relationship screen that excludes conflicted buyers.

#### Exit gate

Todd can state in writing:

- which organizations he may approach;
- which experience and artifacts he may use;
- what data TKO will and will not accept;
- what must be reviewed by counsel or employer policy;
- how Phase 2 remains independent of the Diagnostic recommendation.

#### Commercial value

This is the only true P0. The repository itself flags employment and confidential-information risk. No content or sales activity should outrun this decision.

### Days 1–7 — Assemble the sellable package

#### Actions

- Convert the current Diagnostic and Sprint language into one concise master SOW with two phases.
- Use [`docs/RECOVERY_ASSESSMENT_SAMPLE.md`](docs/RECOVERY_ASSESSMENT_SAMPLE.md) as the structural starting point for a synthetic PA sample.
- Assemble a sample output containing:
  - executive decision;
  - baseline scorecard;
  - current-state / exception map;
  - constraint and dependency register;
  - decision-rights map;
  - target workflow;
  - 90-day roadmap;
  - evidence boundary.
- Consolidate the existing Question Library and current service-page inputs into a kickoff and evidence-request packet.
- Prepare one RachelOS proof walk-through showing queue, memory, facts, gaps, approval, Today, and system health, with explicit “different domain / implementation proof” labeling.
- Prepare a two-page security and evidence-handling note.

#### Exit gate

A qualified buyer can answer:

- What decision will this engagement help us make?
- What exactly happens in 15 days?
- What do we have to provide?
- What will we receive?
- What is excluded?
- Why is Todd credible?
- Why is the Sprint not predetermined?
- How will sensitive evidence be handled?

### Days 4–14 — Run controlled market validation

#### Actions

- Build a small list of warm relationships, referral partners, specialty groups, MSOs, and provider operators that pass the conflict screen.
- Prioritize active triggers: denial / backlog pressure, staffing constraints, vendor or automation decision, payer / specialty variance, centralization, or key-person risk.
- Ask for a working conversation, not generic feedback on the website.
- Use the existing contact qualification model:
  - workflow under pressure;
  - current trigger;
  - executive sponsor;
  - decision timing;
  - action intent within 90 days;
  - evidence availability.
- Record every objection, missing proof request, scope concern, and buyer wording as evidence.
- Do not publish a broad content program as a substitute for direct conversations.

#### Target

- 15–20 carefully selected outreach conversations or referral requests;
- 6–8 qualified working calls;
- 2–3 proposal-worthy opportunities;
- one $100,000 phase-gated proposal.

These are operating targets, not evidence-backed conversion forecasts.

### Days 10–21 — Qualify and shape one proposal

#### Qualification gates

Proceed only when:

- a senior sponsor owns the operating pressure;
- one PA workflow or segment can be bounded;
- leadership must make a decision within 90 days;
- usable de-identified evidence and stakeholder access are plausible;
- the client can fund the $25,000 Diagnostic;
- a $75,000 Sprint is commercially possible if the evidence supports it;
- the scope does not require clinical advice, managed operations, or major production integration;
- conflict and data-handling requirements are acceptable.

#### Proposal controls

- Preserve the fixed Diagnostic boundary.
- State assumptions and required client responsibilities.
- Show the exact deliverable list.
- Define acceptance as delivery and decision readiness, not guaranteed performance.
- Price Phase 2 at $75,000 only for a scope that fits the existing Sprint range.
- Include explicit continue / stop criteria.
- Require a change order for travel, expanded interviews, data remediation, specialists, integrations, or additional workflow families.

### Days 15–30 — Close and mobilize

#### Actions

- Resolve procurement, evidence handling, conflicts, payment, sponsor, and scheduling.
- Schedule Phase 1 only after access and stakeholder conditions are satisfied.
- Invoice according to the current Diagnostic posture: 50% at scheduling and 50% at executive readout, unless the master agreement requires an approved alternative.
- Create a client-specific evidence register and confidentiality boundary before artifacts are collected.
- Begin with the Decision Charter, not a platform demonstration.

#### Definition of commercial success

Best case: signed $100,000 master engagement with Phase 1 funded and Phase 2 subject to the decision gate.

Acceptable first result: signed $25,000 Diagnostic with a contractually clear $75,000 follow-on option and a qualified path to authorization. Do not distort the Diagnostic to claim a $100,000 win prematurely.

## Delivery roadmap after signature

### Phase 1 — Diagnostic

Use the 15-day model in [`04_ASSESSMENT_MODEL.md`](04_ASSESSMENT_MODEL.md).

### Decision gate

The executive brief recommends one of:

- execute internally;
- use or reconfigure an existing vendor;
- engage a specialist partner;
- proceed with the TKO Sprint;
- gather missing evidence;
- defer;
- stop.

### Phase 2 — Sprint

If TKO proceeds:

1. Weeks 1–2: confirm target design, owner, measures, dependencies, and pilot.
2. Weeks 3–5: build standard work, rules, roles, exception paths, and necessary tool requirements / configuration.
3. Weeks 6–8: train and launch the bounded pilot.
4. Weeks 9–10: observe and correct adoption, workflow, and control failures.
5. Weeks 11–12: hand off, define sustainment, and decide whether to scale, revise, or stop.

Custom software is considered only if the Diagnostic proves that existing tools and operating changes cannot close the accepted constraint.

## Engineering roadmap

### Now — zero engineering

Use:

- the current public service pages;
- contact intake and durable lead capture;
- existing RachelOS proof images;
- manual document templates;
- secure client-approved file exchange chosen during contracting;
- manual TIF evidence / asset use only where appropriate.

Only address friction observed in delivery. Likely document-level needs:

- reusable Decision Charter;
- PA evidence register;
- scorecard worksheet;
- finding / constraint / dependency register;
- executive brief template;

Do not automate these until manual use shows stable fields and repeated effort.

Review whether repeated delivery warrants:

- additive assessment templates;
- a manual cross-engagement pattern review;
- narrow TIF links for approved findings and assets;
- a client-safe publication / measurement record;
- a refined, tested maturity rubric.

This is a decision checkpoint, not an implementation commitment.

### Explicitly deferred

- RachelOS scoring or queue generalization;
- new lifecycle or relationship-state engines;
- client assessment portal;
- multi-tenant consulting platform;
- BoundOS;
- graph or vector infrastructure;
- embeddings and semantic retrieval;
- autonomous agents;
- autonomous findings, recommendations, or publication;
- generalized integration layer;
- client-facing TIF;
- maturity benchmark database;
- custom PA workflow software before a Diagnostic proves the need.
- new commercial artifacts that do not answer a repeated buyer objection or delivery need.

## Validation approach

### Commercial validation

Track:

- qualified conversations;
- proposal requests;
- objections by category;
- evidence / security questions;
- proposal-to-Diagnostic conversion;
- Diagnostic decision accepted within ten business days;
- Phase 2 authorization;
- time from first conversation to signature.

Do not infer conversion effectiveness from web traffic alone.

### Method validation

For each engagement:

- trace every material finding to evidence;
- have the sponsor confirm factual accuracy separately from agreement with recommendations;
- use a second reviewer on maturity judgments;
- record what the Diagnostic changed in the client's decision;
- distinguish implementation delivery from activation and validation;
- record client reuse permission explicitly.

### Delivery validation

For the Sprint:

- accept a baseline before change;
- define the pilot and comparison period;
- observe adoption, adherence, exceptions, and failure;
- document competing changes and attribution limits;
- decide scale / revise / stop at handoff.

## Rollback strategy

The first-sale plan is document- and conversation-led, so rollback is straightforward:

- retire or revise the $100,000 packaging without changing the underlying $25,000 Diagnostic;
- stop after Phase 1 if the evidence does not support a Sprint;
- revert to internal execution or an existing vendor if that is the better recommendation;
- withdraw any unsupported public claim and retain its evidence trail;
- isolate or delete client evidence according to the agreed retention policy;
- avoid database or product changes that would require technical rollback.

## Regression risks and controls

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| Broad positioning returns | Keep one public entry offer and one gated follow-on |
| A $100,000 target biases the diagnosis | Separate Phase 2 authorization and allow a stop / partner / internal recommendation |
| Healthcare experience is presented as a TKO client case | Label it advisory experience; use no employer, client, or outcome claim without permission |
| RachelOS is sold as healthcare proof | Label it direct implementation proof in another domain |
| Sensitive or employer information enters TIF | Apply conflict, confidentiality, tenant, and claim-boundary review before capture |
| Maturity scores appear validated | Label directional; expose evidence and confidence; no composite benchmark |
| Engineering distracts from sales | Require evidence from paid delivery before adding a feature |
| Manual delivery becomes inconsistent | Use the standard artifact set and evidence chain; review each engagement |
| Custom work expands beyond one workflow | Enforce scope and change-order gates |
