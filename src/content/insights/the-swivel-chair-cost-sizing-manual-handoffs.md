An operator reads a request, looks up a record, copies an identifier, checks another system, and returns to update the first screen. The judgment may be small; the reconstruction and reconciliation can dominate the work.

Before proposing an integration, identify exactly which part is repeated data movement and which part is a necessary control.

## Observe complete transactions

Sample actual cases across the normal and exception paths. Record active handling time separately from elapsed waiting. Count repeated entry, lookups, retries, corrections, and handoffs without an owner.

An hour of shadowing can identify hypotheses. It does not establish the annual workload or prove that every observed step can be removed.

## Make the assumptions visible

Consider an illustrative estimate: 50,000 annual transactions with four measured minutes of avoidable handling per transaction would represent approximately 3,333 hours. The volume and time are assumptions here, not TKO client results.

That capacity is not automatically a payroll saving. Some time may be redeployed, some may reduce overtime, and some may be too fragmented to recover. Implementation cost, ongoing support, exceptions, and changed controls belong in the business case.

Use a low, expected, and high scenario. State what evidence would change the estimate and who is responsible for validating it.

## Extend before replacing

Check the existing tool's workflow, import, export, and supported integration features. A better field, trigger, or handoff can sometimes remove the defect without a new application.

If a connection is missing, specify the source authority, required input, receiving system, retry behavior, and reconciliation evidence. Include permissions and a manual path when the integration is unavailable.

## Preserve the judgment

Automation can prepare a reconciled work package. A model may draft a summary of the known context. Neither should silently decide which conflicting record is correct or authorize an unapproved change.

The useful result is a measured reduction in repeated work with the required controls still intact. Record rework and exception volume as well as handling time.
