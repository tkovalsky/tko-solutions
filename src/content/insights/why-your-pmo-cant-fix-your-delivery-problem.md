A workstream can report progress accurately while the end-to-end release remains uncertain. The team completed its assigned task; the receiving team still needs a rule, record, approval, or decision that was not in that task.

The issue is not that a PMO cannot help. It is whether the program's reporting captures the decisions and handoffs required to make the work executable.

## Connect the status to an artifact

For a reported milestone, ask which artifact supports it, which scenario it proves, and who accepted it. An application test, approved policy interpretation, and a demonstrated downstream handoff establish different things.

Keep the reporting cadence that helps the team. Add the evidence needed for the release decision instead of discarding the whole dashboard.

## Track decisions alongside tasks

A task can remain blocked while its real prerequisite is an unmade business decision. Record that decision explicitly: the owner, options, affected scope, and date needed.

An overdue task is a signal. It does not explain whether more capacity, a changed requirement, a vendor action, or an accountable leadership decision is required.

## Inspect the boundary between teams

Take one scenario across the approved obligation, source authorities, workflow, systems, controls, and operations. Check what the next team receives and how it responds to incomplete or rejected input.

The [synthetic decision lab](/healthcare/impact-assessment) shows why policy determination, UM routing, and downstream claims evidence remain separate. Its platform-gap explanation is simulated, not an executed failure in a payer integration.

## Give leadership a concrete next action

A useful readiness readout links each gap to its owner, dependency, proposed change, and acceptance evidence. It says what can proceed, what is still uncertain, and which decision leadership must make next.
