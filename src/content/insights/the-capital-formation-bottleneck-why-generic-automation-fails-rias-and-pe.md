An investor or prospective client asks a specific question. The answer may be in approved material, but the relationship history is spread across notes and conversations. A generic update does not resolve the question; a bespoke draft can still misstate the facts.

Start with the question the person actually asked, the source material your firm permits, and the reviewer responsible for the response.

## Wealth advisory and capital formation are different workflows

A wealth advisory inquiry, an existing client's service request, and an institutional investor update have different owners and review requirements. Do not treat them as one universal automation sequence.

For each workflow, identify the permitted audience, approved content, contact permissions, required review, and where the completed touch is recorded. The firm's qualified compliance and professional owners determine the applicable requirements.

## Carry the question forward

In a fictional example, a prospective advisory client asks how the firm approaches liquidity needs. The operator records that question and links the approved explanation the client can review. An open question about timing remains open until confirmed.

A work view shows the context, the next commitment, and who owns it. Anonymous content consumption does not establish the visitor's identity, financial circumstances, or suitability.

## A draft is a proposal

A model can help prepare wording from allowed records. Keep any proposed claim traceable to its approved source. The responsible reviewer checks the actual message, including figures and commitments, before use.

## Build around your firm's controls

The first scope should name the content and inquiry path, supported systems, reviewer, exception handling, and handoff. Define how failures are surfaced and how contact preferences are honored. Measure completed follow-up, corrections, waiting, and the quality of the recorded context.
