A client asks a question outside the renewal process. The record is saved, but no owner or follow-up commitment appears in the broker's working day.

That is a specific defect you can count and fix. It does not prove why a client leaves or justify a promise that automation will increase renewals.

## Start with the existing agency workflow

Inspect what the agency management system and CRM already support: activities, assignments, reminders, templates, and approved integrations. Do not assume that a system holding policy records has no workflow capability.

Identify the missing connection between the client's question, the responsible broker, the relevant record, and the next commitment.

## Keep the source of advice explicit

A news event or model-generated suggestion is not enough to establish that a client has a coverage gap. The qualified professional must inspect the authoritative records and determine what discussion is appropriate.

In a fictional example, a client asks whether a business change requires a review. The system can surface that recorded question and the due action. It should not infer an unverified exposure or recommend a policy change on its own.

## Prepare a reviewable touchpoint

Where model assistance is permitted, prepare draft wording from recorded context and approved material. Show the relevant sources and unresolved questions alongside the exact message.

The broker checks the interpretation, recipient, tone, and contact permissions before authorizing outreach. An approval interface is not a promise of E&O protection or compliance.

## Measure the follow-through

Count commitments with no owner, unanswered questions, overdue actions, corrections, and completed touches with recorded outcomes. Measure retention separately if the agency has sufficient evidence to evaluate it; do not infer that result from activity volume.
