The team knows a workflow is difficult. A funding decision needs more detail: where the work waits, how much repeated handling occurs, which outcomes matter, and what a first change would cost.

The aim is not to produce a more dramatic ROI number. It is to make the proposed change and its assumptions inspectable.

## Establish a starting measure

Choose one workflow and sample real cases. Separate active handling, elapsed waiting, corrections, exceptions, and missed commitments. Record the population, period, and data gaps.

Time released from a task is capacity, not automatically cash savings. An avoided hire is a scenario dependent on future workload and staffing decisions. Distinguish each from an expense that will actually leave the budget.

## State the causal assumption

A proposed integration may remove repeated entry. It may also introduce reconciliation, support, and exception work. Show why the change is expected to affect the measure and what might prevent that effect.

Use low, expected, and high scenarios. Include implementation fees, ongoing service, third-party costs, review effort, and the work retained in the manual path. Identify who validates the financial assumptions.

## Fund a release with acceptance evidence

The first scope should specify the controlling rules, source authorities, accountable owner, failure behavior, and criteria for accepting the work. A smaller release can test the hypothesis before a wider rollout.

For a payer transition, the evidence may include the decision trace, an emitted control artifact, the receiving system's acknowledgement, and observed downstream behavior. For client follow-up, it may include captured commitments, completed actions, corrections, and recorded outcomes.

## Keep model assistance in its role

A model can help draft an explanation from approved findings. It should not create the baseline, decide which assumption is true, or convert an estimate into a promised result. The sponsor and finance owner retain the decision.
