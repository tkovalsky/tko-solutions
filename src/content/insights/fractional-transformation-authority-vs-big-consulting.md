A complex program can need more delivery capacity and a clearer operating model at the same time. The useful choice is which gap is blocking the next release.

An integrator may be the right partner for a large implementation. An independent principal can be useful when leadership needs a bounded, evidence-backed answer across teams and vendors before committing more work.

## Identify what is missing

If the requirement is clear and a workstream lacks available engineering or operations capacity, add the appropriate capacity.

If teams disagree on the controlling rule, the receiving system's responsibility, or the evidence required to release, another team may inherit the same uncertainty. Resolve that uncertainty as specific work with a named decision owner.

## Define the principal's authority

A written scope should distinguish examining the operating path, recommending a backlog, coordinating an agreed decision, and owning production delivery. These responsibilities are not interchangeable.

For TKO's readiness sprint, the client's accountable executives, compliance owners, architects, and delivery teams retain their authorities. I inspect one approved mandate or transition, connect its obligations to the affected workflows and systems, and produce a readout of demonstrated evidence and open decisions.

## Make the deliverable usable

The engagement should end with work the next team can execute: a source-linked obligation register, cross-system impact map, gaps and dependencies, accountable ownership, business rules, exception paths, and acceptance criteria.

The sponsor needs to see where a claim of readiness is supported and where it is still an assertion. A report without an owner for the next decision leaves the program in the same place.

## Match the size of the help to the work

TKO is a principal-led practice with limited capacity, not a substitute for a large implementation team. Follow-on delivery governance or additional builds require an explicit scope and availability decision.
