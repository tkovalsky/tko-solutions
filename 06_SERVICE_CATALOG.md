# TKO Service Catalog

**Commercial principle:** One clear entry offer, one evidence-gated implementation path, and a small set of modules
**Current public market:** Provider-side prior authorization
**Catalog rule:** A reusable method is not automatically a market-ready offer

## 1. Prior Authorization Performance Diagnostic

### Problem solved

Leadership cannot reliably explain what is driving prior-authorization delay, rework, denials, backlog, variation, or staff dependency and is at risk of making a premature staffing, vendor, workflow, or automation decision.

### Expected outcomes

- one accepted evidence base;
- addressable drivers separated from symptoms and unsupported assumptions;
- explicit decision rights, exception paths, and dependency risks;
- target workflow and operating requirements;
- responsible decision to fix, fund, automate, use an existing vendor, investigate, defer, or stop.

### Inputs

- one sponsor and bounded workflow;
- six to ten stakeholder interviews;
- appropriately de-identified operating examples;
- available volume, denial, turnaround, payer, specialty, backlog, touch, effort, and staffing evidence;
- workflow, standard-work, escalation, training, and vendor artifacts.

### Outputs

- executive decision brief;
- baseline scorecard and KPI definitions;
- current-state workflow and exception map;
- denial, rework, delay, and handoff driver analysis;
- decision-rights and escalation map;
- key-person dependency register;
- target workflow;
- prioritized backlog;
- 90-day roadmap.

### Duration and buyer

15 business days; $25,000 fixed. Buyers include provider-operations, patient-access, revenue-cycle, PA / UM, medical-group, and MSO leaders.

### Supporting platform components

- TKO qualified intake and evidence-safe public scope;
- RachelOS proof of governed facts, state, priority, approval, and action;
- TIF evidence and asset traceability for internal production;
- assessment, question, framework, and sample-deliverable libraries.

The client does not need to use any TKO software.

## 2. 90-Day Prior Authorization Improvement Sprint

### Problem solved

An accepted diagnostic exists, but the organization needs help turning one priority into operating behavior rather than another recommendation deck.

### Expected outcomes

- one bounded target workflow activated;
- standard work, roles, decision rights, exceptions, and escalation operating;
- critical payer / specialty knowledge maintainable by the team;
- a small accepted KPI set and review cadence;
- a controlled pilot with adoption and early operating evidence;
- team handoff and scale / revise / stop decision.

### Inputs

- accepted Diagnostic or equivalent evidence base;
- one prioritized workflow or segment;
- executive sponsor and client owner;
- target measures and improvement hypothesis;
- access to frontline, workflow, technology, compliance, and adjacent stakeholders.

### Outputs

- implemented playbook and SOPs;
- roles and decision-rights matrix;
- exception taxonomy and escalation design;
- shared operating-knowledge artifacts;
- scorecard specification / configuration;
- pilot backlog and issue log;
- bounded automation requirements or prototype only where approved;
- training, handoff, and 30-day sustainment plan.

### Duration and buyer

12 weeks; $65,000–$95,000. Buyer is the sponsor who accepts the Diagnostic and can assign a client owner.

### Supporting platform components

RachelOS supplies design evidence for canonical queues, missing-fact detection, human approval, operational memory, and system health. It is not deployed as the client's platform. Existing client systems should be configured or extended first.

## 3. Prior Authorization Performance Recovery Program

### Problem solved

A buyer wants one procurement vehicle that covers diagnosis and a bounded improvement while preserving a formal evidence gate.

### Expected outcomes

All Diagnostic outcomes plus one activated, measured improvement and a scale / stop decision.

### Inputs and outputs

The combined inputs and outputs of Services 1 and 2.

### Duration and buyer

Approximately 15 weeks. Proposed fee: $100,000, structured as:

- Phase 1: $25,000 Diagnostic;
- Phase 2: $75,000 Sprint, authorized only after the Phase 1 decision gate.

Likely buyer: a specialty medical group, MSO, provider-side operations organization, or a bounded business unit inside a larger healthcare organization.

### Supporting platform components

No additional platform component is required. This is commercial packaging of current services.

### Commercial control

The master SOW must allow the client and TKO to stop after Phase 1. TKO's recommendation cannot be contractually forced toward its own Sprint.

## 4. Healthcare Program Recovery Assessment

### Problem solved

A sponsor sees a stalled or misleadingly green program but lacks visibility into cross-team constraints, dependencies, capacity, and unresolved decisions.

### Expected outcomes

- actual vs. reported state;
- top enterprise constraints;
- dependency and decision visibility;
- prioritized recovery sequence;
- decision about stabilization, rescoping, funding, escalation, or stop.

### Inputs

Program artifacts, status reports, plans, risk logs, decision records, stakeholder interviews, and recent examples of missed or delayed work.

### Outputs

Constraint register, dependency map, decision register, capacity / governance findings, operating-review design, and recovery roadmap.

### Duration and buyer

One to three weeks depending on program boundary. Likely buyer: COO, CIO, transformation executive, PMO leader, or executive sponsor.

### Supporting platform components

Recovery Assessment playbook, Operational Truth questions, Transformation Recovery dimensions, and RachelOS's distinction between state, priority, recommendation, and action.

### Commercial control

Offer selectively through trusted relationships. The repository contains experience-backed credibility but no attributed TKO program-recovery case.

## 5. Decision Rights & Exception Routing Sprint

### Problem solved

Routine work moves, but exceptions wait, vary by operator, or escalate through informal relationships.

### Expected outcomes

Explicit authority, predictable escalation, maintainable exception categories, visible rationale, and reduced person dependency.

### Inputs

Decision examples, exception samples, policies, roles, escalation records, current standard work, and stakeholder interviews.

### Outputs

Decision inventory, authority matrix, exception taxonomy, escalation ladder, backup map, service expectations, and auditability requirements.

### Duration and buyer

One to two weeks after an assessment. Buyer: operations, PA / UM, compliance, transformation, or service-delivery leader.

### Supporting platform components

Human-approval patterns, source-authority design, decision and exception frameworks, and audit-trail patterns.

## 6. Administrative Burden Diagnostic

### Problem solved

Leadership sees staffing pressure but does not know whether effort is consumed by necessary review, poor intake, waiting, rework, status reconstruction, or unstable exception handling.

### Expected outcomes

A defensible burden anatomy and a decision about removal, standardization, role change, tool configuration, or automation.

### Inputs

Work samples, touch / time evidence, queue data, interviews, rework examples, and exception paths.

### Outputs

Burden heat map, touch and waiting analysis, rework drivers, capacity constraints, and prioritized removal / standardization opportunities.

### Duration and buyer

Normally embedded in the 15-day Diagnostic. Buyer: operations, revenue-cycle, patient-access, workforce, or PA leader.

### Supporting platform components

Healthcare experience library, PA operating-quality framework, workflow mapping, and measurable-baseline method.

## 7. AI / Automation Readiness Review

### Problem solved

Leadership is deciding whether to automate a workflow or assumes an implemented capability is delivering value without production evidence.

### Expected outcomes

Clear separation of workflow, governance, activation, adoption, and technology constraints; a build / buy / defer / stop decision.

### Inputs

Workflow, source records, production configuration, operating records, failure logs, adoption evidence, policies, and decision examples.

### Outputs

Built / Activated / Validated map, source-authority analysis, human-control design, exception and failure-path review, ranked constraints, and decision recommendation.

### Duration and buyer

Embedded in a Diagnostic or Program Recovery Assessment. Buyer: COO, CIO, CDO, transformation, or operations leader.

### Supporting platform components

RachelOS implementation evidence, AI Delivery Assessment method, governance constitution, operational health, and approval-gated workflows.

### Commercial control

Do not sell model selection, prompt engineering, autonomous-agent strategy, or generalized AI transformation.

## 8. Executive Operating Review Design

### Problem solved

Executive meetings receive status but do not reliably surface constraints, decisions, ownership, evidence, and closure.

### Expected outcomes

A repeatable operating review that turns current state into decisions and accountable follow-through.

### Inputs

Current agenda, dashboards, status packs, action logs, risk / issue records, and decision examples.

### Outputs

Review agenda, constraint register, decision register, escalation rules, KPI dictionary, ownership model, and review cadence.

### Duration and buyer

One to two weeks, often as a follow-on module. Buyer: COO, program sponsor, PMO, transformation, or operations leader.

### Supporting platform components

Canonical queue, explainable priority, decision register, operating telemetry, and Recovery Assessment methods.

## Services to defer

| Service idea | Why defer |
|---|---|
| General Operational Intelligence Platform | No multi-client product proof, integrations, security model, or repeatable adoption evidence |
| BoundOS | Future product hypothesis, not a mature offering |
| TIF as SaaS | Internal evidence / asset infrastructure with incomplete publication and measurement loops |
| Managed Prior Authorization | Outside repository scope and operating model |
| Enterprise AI Transformation | Too broad; proof supports governed workflow intervention, not an enterprise category |
| Fractional advisory | Plausible after a delivered engagement, but it weakens the current bounded offer and has no present client pull |
| Claims / eligibility / adjudication services | Insufficient direct evidence; clinical and transaction-processing boundaries are explicit |

## Catalog governance

Add a service only when it has:

1. a named buyer and trigger;
2. a bounded decision and workflow;
3. evidence-backed credibility;
4. defined inputs, outputs, duration, and exclusions;
5. a sample deliverable;
6. a safe data-handling position;
7. a measurable success definition;
8. a reason it cannot be handled as a module in an existing service.
