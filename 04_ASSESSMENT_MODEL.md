# TKO Assessment Model

**Flagship:** Prior Authorization Performance Diagnostic
**Current commercial terms:** 15 business days, $25,000 fixed fee
**Assessment rule:** Diagnose an executive decision and a bounded workflow—not a software category

## Why this is the flagship

Several assessments appear in the repository, but only one now has all of the following:

- a specific buyer and urgent operating problem;
- a current public page;
- fixed price, duration, scope, and exclusions;
- detailed inputs, activities, outputs, and success criteria;
- a defined implementation follow-on;
- healthcare domain credibility;
- supporting workflow, governance, and evidence methods;
- a plausible route to a $100,000 phased engagement.

That assessment is the **Prior Authorization Performance Diagnostic**.

The broader Program Recovery, Workflow Intelligence, Decision Governance, Operational Maturity, and AI Readiness concepts should be used as modules or selectively packaged assessments—not marketed as an undifferentiated catalog.

## 1. Flagship assessment: Prior Authorization Performance Diagnostic

### Problem solved

Leadership sees delay, denial, backlog, rework, inconsistency, or staff dependency but lacks a trusted fact base for deciding whether to change workflow, staffing, policy operation, vendor configuration, automation, or ownership.

### Likely buyer

- COO or VP Operations;
- revenue-cycle or patient-access executive;
- prior-authorization / utilization-management leader;
- specialty medical group or MSO executive;
- transformation sponsor with authority to fund a 90-day change.

### Standard boundary

- one organization;
- one defined PA workflow, payer / specialty segment, location, or team;
- one executive sponsor;
- up to ten stakeholder interviews;
- de-identified operating evidence;
- remote by default;
- no production-system access required for standard scope.

### Questions answered

1. Where does a case first become incomplete, delayed, or likely to require rework?
2. Which denial and exception patterns are operationally addressable?
3. How much staff effort is consumed by touches, follow-up, escalation, and reconstruction?
4. Which payer, specialty, location, or evidence patterns explain meaningful variation?
5. Where are decision rights, ownership, or escalation rules unclear?
6. Which knowledge dependencies create continuity and capacity risk?
7. What should be standardized, measured, automated, stopped, or retained under human judgment?
8. What can responsibly improve in the next 90 days?

### Inputs

- available volumes, approval / denial records, reason categories, turnaround, payer and specialty mix;
- queue, backlog, aged-exception, productivity, and staffing evidence;
- current workflows, SOPs, training, job aids, escalation rules, and vendor reports;
- six to ten stakeholder interviews across leadership, management, frontline operations, and adjacent functions;
- appropriately de-identified case examples;
- current initiatives, vendor decisions, or automation hypotheses.

Absence or poor quality of evidence is itself a finding; it must not be converted into a false score.

### 15-business-day delivery sequence

| Period | Work | Outputs |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–3 | Frame decision, scope, evidence boundary, stakeholder set, baseline, and definitions | Decision Charter; Evidence Register; initial KPI dictionary |
| Days 4–8 | Interview stakeholders and trace intake, evidence, routing, payer variation, exceptions, escalation, follow-up, and rework | Current-state map; exception map; dependency inventory |
| Days 9–11 | Analyze causes, variation, burden, decision rights, staff dependency, and automation readiness | Findings; baseline scorecard; constraint and dependency registers |
| Days 12–14 | Design the target workflow and prioritize the 90-day change | Target workflow; decision-rights map; prioritized backlog; KPI set |
| Day 15 | Present evidence, tradeoffs, recommendation, and required decision | Executive readout and decision brief |

### Required deliverables

1. Executive Decision Brief
2. Baseline Scorecard and KPI Definitions
3. Current-State Workflow and Exception Map
4. Denial, Rework, Handoff, and Delay Driver Analysis
5. Decision-Rights and Escalation Map
6. Key-Person Dependency Register
7. Built / Activated / Validated Map for relevant tools and automation
8. Target Workflow and Operating Requirements
9. Prioritized Improvement Backlog
10. 90-Day Roadmap with owners, measures, dependencies, and decision gates
11. Evidence / Limitation Appendix

### Success criteria

The Diagnostic is successful when:

- leadership accepts one bounded fact base;
- the top operating causes and evidence limitations are explicit;
- the accountable owner and next decision are named;
- target measures have defined sources and boundaries;
- leadership decides within ten business days to fix, fund, automate, use an existing vendor, gather evidence, defer, or stop.

The Diagnostic does not require the client to retain TKO for implementation.

### Exclusions

- PHI ingestion by default;
- clinical criteria or medical-necessity decisions;
- legal, regulatory, or coding advice;
- payer contracting;
- claims adjudication;
- production integration;
- vendor procurement;
- custom software;
- guaranteed denial, cost, staffing, reimbursement, or revenue outcomes.

## 2. Standard diagnostic scorecard

Scores are optional aids, never substitutes for findings. Every score must display evidence availability and rationale.

### Operating-performance indicators

Select only metrics with available, agreed definitions:

- touches per case;
- staff minutes per case;
- clean-submission rate;
- first-pass approval;
- avoidable denial rate;
- median turnaround time;
- aged exceptions;
- escalation cycle time;
- rework / resubmission rate;
- workflow adherence;
- training completion and adoption.

### Operating-condition dimensions

Use the maturity dimensions in [`05_MATURITY_MODEL.md`](05_MATURITY_MODEL.md) directionally:

- governance;
- workflow;
- decision quality;
- automation readiness;
- AI trust and control;
- data quality and source authority;
- operational visibility;
- measurement;
- adoption and change;
- knowledge transferability.

Do not average the two groups into a single “health” number. Performance indicators describe observed results; maturity dimensions describe operating conditions.

## 3. Assessment modules already present

### A. Decision Rights and Exception Routing Review

**Use when:** cases move inconsistently, overrides are unclear, or escalation depends on personal relationships.

**Outputs:** decision inventory, authority matrix, exception taxonomy, backup / escalation map, auditability gaps.

**Commercial disposition:** delivery-ready module; potential one- to two-week focused sprint after a Diagnostic.

### B. Administrative Burden Diagnostic

**Use when:** leadership attributes pressure to headcount but does not know where touches, waiting, correction, and reconstruction occur.

**Outputs:** burden heat map, touch / wait analysis, rework drivers, capacity constraints, removal / standardization opportunities.

**Commercial disposition:** module within the PA Diagnostic or another bounded healthcare workflow.

### C. Healthcare Program Recovery Assessment

**Use when:** an executive sponsor cannot distinguish local progress from enterprise delivery health.

**Outputs:** actual-vs-reported state, constraint register, dependency map, decision register, capacity / governance findings, recovery sequence.

**Commercial disposition:** selectively sell to a warm buyer where the sponsor, program, decision, and evidence boundary are explicit. Do not displace the PA flagship in public positioning.

### D. AI / Automation Readiness Review

**Use when:** leadership is considering automation or believes a capability is already “done.”

**Outputs:** Built / Activated / Validated map, source-authority review, workflow-stability gate, human-control design, health / failure visibility, build / buy / defer / stop recommendation.

**Commercial disposition:** embedded module. Do not revive the standalone AI Delivery Assessment as the primary offer.

### E. Workflow Intelligence Assessment

**Use when:** records exist but there is no reliable view of state, priority, next action, or missing facts.

**Outputs:** signal / memory / fact / state map, gap analysis, queue and decision requirements, target operating loop.

**Commercial disposition:** generalized description of the method, not a public offer until a bounded non-PA demand pattern and client proof exist.

### F. Regulatory Workflow Governance Review

**Use when:** a requirement is documented but leadership cannot show how it operates in daily decisions, evidence, exceptions, and oversight.

**Outputs:** requirement-to-workflow trace, governance control stack, evidence and decision gaps, review cadence.

**Commercial disposition:** experience-backed module; explicitly not legal advice, audit, or certification.

## 4. Assessments that should not be separate products now

| Historical concept | Recommended disposition |
|---|---|
| Operational Recovery Assessment | Preserve the method and rapid five-day playbook; merge public entry into the current Diagnostic |
| Human API Risk Assessment | Use as a dependency lens and register, not an offer |
| Gold Card Assessment | Treat readiness as one result of PA operating quality |
| AI Delivery Assessment | Use as an embedded readiness module |
| Operational Maturity Assessment | Use the maturity model to structure findings; do not sell certification |
| Enterprise Value / Exit Readiness | Defer until buyer demand and evidence are established |
| Claims / Eligibility / Benefits Assessment | Discovery only; repository lacks sufficient direct proof |

## 5. Assessment evidence rules

1. Interview statements require a named role, date, context, and corroboration status.
2. A dashboard is a representation; source records and observed behavior may contradict it.
3. Missing data is reported as **Untracked**, not zero.
4. One example may identify a risk but not establish prevalence.
5. A directional score must expose its evidence and rationale.
6. Financial and outcome estimates must show assumptions and ranges.
7. Client-confidential evidence is not reusable public proof without explicit permission.
8. AI-generated analysis is a draft; a human owns each final finding and recommendation.

## 6. Assessment-to-Sprint decision gate

The Sprint may proceed only when:

- the baseline and limitation statement are accepted;
- one workflow or segment is prioritized;
- an executive sponsor and client owner are accountable;
- the improvement hypothesis is evidence-backed;
- target measures and sources are defined;
- dependencies and specialist needs are known;
- sensitive-data and access controls are agreed;
- the client accepts the possibility of revise / stop if pilot evidence is weak.

## 7. Flagship assessment acceptance test

Before offering the Diagnostic, confirm that a buyer can inspect:

- a sample executive brief;
- a sample workflow / exception map;
- a sample baseline scorecard with synthetic or anonymized data;
- a sample constraint and dependency register;
- the exact interview and artifact request;
- the schedule, price, exclusions, and evidence boundary;
- Todd's bounded healthcare credibility;
- RachelOS proof as method evidence, clearly labeled as another domain.
