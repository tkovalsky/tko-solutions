TKO Solutions is a principal-led **Healthcare Transformation & Operating Model Advisory** practice. Todd Kovalsky is positioned as a **Healthcare Transformation & Operating Model Strategist** who makes complex, regulated healthcare change executable.

The core promise is:

> Reduce the burden. Preserve the control. Redesign the system.

TKO meets buyers at operational pain: administrative burden, provider abrasion, fragmented workflows, hidden dependencies, regulatory pressure, stalled transformation, ineffective automation, and programs that are moving without converging.

The scalable capability is structuring complexity. Prior authorization, utilization management, provider operations, interoperability, enterprise delivery, RachelOS, and CRE are evidence domains—not the company category.

## Founder Identity

Todd's archetype is **Strategist + Translator + Orchestrator + Operator**.

- Strategist: converts ambiguity into a transformation thesis, target model, and roadmap.
- Translator: connects operations, healthcare, technology, controls, risk, and implementation.
- Orchestrator: creates integrated execution where no team owns the whole picture.
- Operator: understands what breaks between strategy and implementation and can work with the systems underneath the strategy.

Core founder description:

> Todd helps healthcare organizations translate complex operational change into executable operating models, technology roadmaps, and transformation programs—particularly where regulatory requirements, administrative burden, provider experience, and enterprise technology collide.

## Point of View

Healthcare organizations cannot eliminate burden by indiscriminately eliminating controls. The work is to determine which controls are necessary, which can be risk-adjusted, which work can be eliminated or automated, where human judgment remains necessary, and how the new model is implemented across a complex enterprise.

The AI thesis is equally specific: the hard part is not calling a model; it is redesigning the surrounding decision system.

Signals → structured facts → current state → priority → recommendation → human review when needed → action → outcome → feedback.

## Offer Ladder

Public pages show starting prices only. Upper ranges, effort economics, and revenue targets remain internal.

1. **Executive Diagnostic** — 1–2 weeks, starting at $5K. A tightly bounded paid entry point for one problem, limited interviews, artifact review, findings, and an executive readout.
2. **Transformation Diagnostic** — 2–3 weeks, starting at $10K. The standard entry offer; includes current-state model, friction and dependency analysis, risks, value hypothesis, interventions, and roadmap. Program recovery is a use case within this offer.
3. **Operating Model & Transformation Design Sprint** — 4–6 weeks, starting at $20K. Produces the target operating model, future-state workflows, decision rights, controls, governance, technology implications, roadmap, KPIs, and business case.
4. **Principal Transformation Advisor** — three-month initial term, starting at $8K/month. Senior advisory without routine PM administration, ticket ownership, or unlimited availability.
5. **Transformation Leadership & Implementation Advisory** — starting at $20K/month or fixed-project scope. Reserved for material responsibility for execution architecture, recovery, orchestration, and implementation governance.

Internal commercial objective: build toward $20K–$40K/month in outside consulting revenue through a small number of high-value enterprise relationships. The immediate objective is one credible $5K–$10K paid diagnostic, then a second engagement and evidence-based expansion.

## Methodologies

- **Administrative Friction Diagnostic:** classify work as necessary, automatable, delegatable, or eliminable and make its economic impact visible.
- **Risk-Adjusted Operations:** make oversight proportional to demonstrated risk and performance.
- **Friction Economics:** connect volume, effort, rework, delay, escalation, appeal, provider burden, and member impact to investment decisions.
- **Transformation Dependency Model:** expose business, application, integration, release, policy, funding, and ownership dependencies.
- **Governed Decision Systems:** design signal, evidence, decision, guardrail, action, exception, human intervention, outcome, and feedback as one operating loop.

## Information and CTA Architecture

Canonical public routes: `/`, `/healthcare`, `/services`, `/approach`, `/selected-work`, `/insights`, `/founder`, and `/contact`.

Primary CTA: **Discuss a Transformation**. Secondary CTA: **Start with a Diagnostic**. The readiness check remains a targeted resource rather than a top-level category.

The contact path asks what is changing, what is stuck, where burden appears, which teams and systems are involved, what has been tried, and what happens if nothing changes. It does not request sensitive or patient information.

## Content and Design Principles

Use direct buyer language, strong hierarchy, editorial sections, concise evidence, and diagrams only when they clarify a real relationship. Avoid abstract category creation, generic AI language, invented frameworks, résumé chronology as the main proof, stock consulting imagery, and SaaS feature-grid density.

The visual character remains serious, senior, analytical, and modern. Preserve the existing palette and typography while using fewer cards, more whitespace, and clearer process and evidence structures.
