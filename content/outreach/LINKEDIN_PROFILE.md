## About (about 1,600 characters)

> Most businesses don't have a tool problem. They have a "what happens next" problem.
>
> The CRM holds the records. The inbox holds the conversations. Spreadsheets hold the
> exceptions. And one or two people, often the owner, hold the judgment that connects them. When
> those people are busy or away, leads go quiet, handoffs stall, and decisions wait.
>
> That's the work I do through TKO Solutions. I find where the work actually breaks down, then
> build the smallest system that fixes it, on the tools you already use. A person stays in charge
> of anything that reaches a client.
>
> The proof I can show you is RachelOS: a system I designed, built, and still run for an
> independent South Florida real-estate operation. It produces one ranked list each morning of
> who needs attention and why, keeps the context in one place, and drafts follow-ups that a
> person approves before they go out. When AI follow-up got facts wrong, I switched it off.
> That's how I think about AI in operations.
>
> Before that, I spent 20+ years in operations, product ownership, and large transformation
> programs: loan settlement and fund operations, advisor and CRM platforms, healthcare
> interoperability, and multi-team healthcare programs. Every one of them taught the same lesson:
> information isn't the same as action.
>
> I work with:
> • Advisory and service firms that want their own follow-up system, not another tool
> • Healthcare teams turning a policy or regulatory change into working operations
>
> I do the work myself and take on a small number of clients.
> → tko.solutions
